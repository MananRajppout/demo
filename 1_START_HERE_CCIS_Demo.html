Why there are two results, not one. The score tells you how good the documentation is. The signature decision tells you whether it can be billed. They are deliberately separate: a note can score 96 and still be blocked for one missing signature, because an auditor does not average — they find the missing element.
Every finding is traceable. The code on each finding (SAI-14, PCP-14, PSS-07) is the rule number in your Compliance Rule Book, and the source line names the policy it comes from. Nothing the system flags is a matter of opinion — you can look it up.

Summary

Fifteen charts were sampled at random on 1 July and every document within them scored against the Compliance Rule Book. Overall documentation quality improved for the second consecutive month, driven by better Person-Centered Plan objectives. SAIOP group notes remain the principal exposure.

Findings requiring correction

  • SAIOP group notes (11 documents). Narrative describes group content without evidencing the individual beneficiary's participation. These units are difficult to defend if reviewed.
  • Expired Person-Centered Plans (5 beneficiaries). Services delivered after the plan's review date lapsed. Highest-priority item this month.
  • Cloned narrative (6 notes, 2 across different beneficiaries). Cross-beneficiary reuse escalated to the Clinical Director separately.
  • Peer Support notes (4 documents). Activity documented as transport or social contact without a recovery-oriented function tied to a PCP goal.

Recommended actions

  • Deliver targeted SAIOP documentation training covering SAI-14 and SAI-15 before the next billing cycle.
  • Complete plan reviews for the five affected beneficiaries and suspend further billing until updated.
  • Hold individual supervision with the two clinicians associated with cross-beneficiary cloning.

Trend

Agency compliance has moved from 79 in May to 83 in June to 87 in July. Blocked documents fell from 41 to 23 over the same period. The improvement follows the introduction of pre-signature review rather than retrospective chart audit.

Rule library — and how other states would work

The 129 checks are tiered. Universal rules hold anywhere; state rules are specific to North Carolina; payer rules are Trillium's additions on top. This is what makes the system extendable beyond one state without rebuilding it.

TIER 1

Universal

Documentation principles that hold in every state: identification elements, signatures and credentials, intervention specificity, beneficiary response, cloned language, plan-to-note linkage.

55
rules · port to any state unchanged
TIER 2

State

Service definitions, eligibility criteria, age limits, weekly hour ranges, service order authority, unit rules, licensure scope. Rewritten per state.

61
rules · North Carolina
TIER 3

Payer / plan

Overlays imposed by a specific LME/MCO or managed care plan above the state baseline. Configured per payer, per document.

How multi-state works. The rule set applied to a document is determined by the state in which the provider is licensed and Medicaid-enrolled, the payer being billed, and the service definition — not by the beneficiary's zip code. Zip is used to propose the correct state at setup and to flag when a beneficiary's location looks inconsistent with the configured jurisdiction. Adding a state means writing its Tier 2 rules; the 55 universal rules carry over unchanged.
